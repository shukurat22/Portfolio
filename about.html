About

The person who keeps the SDLC on track.

Not a checklist of Scrum ceremonies. A Project Manager / Product Owner who sits at the centre of the technology team and makes sure what ships matches what was actually asked for.

Every product I've delivered moves through the same handoff: developers building it, designers shaping it, QA proving it, stakeholders signing off on it. And me, making sure none of those handoffs drops the ball.

I'm a Project Manager / Product Owner with a focus on overseeing the software development lifecycle, from the first requirements conversation through to the release that goes out the door. In practice, that means running point across a full technology team: front-end and back-end developers building the product, UI/UX designers shaping how it looks and works, QA and testers confirming it holds up, and product owners and client stakeholders defining what "done" actually means. My job is keeping all of them moving toward the same outcome, on the same timeline, without anyone having to chase anyone else for a status update.

I run that process using Agile: sprint planning, backlogs, standups, retros. But the actual work is bigger than the ceremonies. It's translating a business need into requirements a team can build against, staying close enough to implementation to catch drift before it becomes a problem, and validating what's shipped through UAT and structured testing rather than taking a developer's word for it.

I've run this across fintech platforms, a proptech mortgage product, and an enterprise brand portal built for a financial services client, and I hold a Certified ScrumMaster® credential to back the process up. The best way to understand how I work isn't to hear me describe it. It's to see it.

  • 01I sit at the center of the SDLC, coordinating developers, designers, QA, and stakeholders toward one shipped outcome, not just running ceremonies.
  • 02I've delivered across fintech, proptech, and financial services: categories where "close enough" isn't good enough, and a missed requirement has real consequences.
  • 03I've led delivery inside funded, cross-functional teams and built process from scratch as a volunteer and capstone lead. I don't need a mature system handed to me to run one.
